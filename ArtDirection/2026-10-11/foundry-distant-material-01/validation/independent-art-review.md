## Findings

The existing raised arch frames now separate clearly from their recessed bays. Cap planes and sidewalls read more convincingly as architectural volume, and the finer masonry avoids the previous near-black blank mass. Course direction remains coherent across the inspected faces. No conspicuous new checker pattern, hard texture seam, upper-tier transparency, floating cutoff or actor obstruction was observed. Lower atmospheric fade and the background hierarchy remain legible. Hero, range ring, working equipment and foreground lanterns retain their visual priority.

The gain is clearer in the skyline detail than in the complete map. Portrait mortar contrast is slightly stronger but does not overpower the actor. The shafts retain rigid regular silhouettes, while the broken span still has long square-ended stubs. Those inherited shape limits remain visibly simpler than the reference and are not resolved by this material pass.

Still images do not establish motion shimmer, crowded-combat readability, physical-device quality or performance.

## Next substantive priority

Prefer a whole northwest production-yard composition over another distant color or trim pass. Current equipment is concentrated on narrow exterior shelves while the transfer-circle area remains broadly empty. A loading/staging group, casting/stock group and cooling group could give the west yard the purposeful density of the reference while preserving a clear loop to the pit and court. Raised props on currently walkable floor require matching shared navigation/collision footprints; they must not be introduced as decoration-only obstacles. Start with a complete yard layout proof showing blocked footprints and hero-scale circulation, then integrate the chosen composition as one coherent slice.

If the walking contract must remain completely unchanged, the alternative is an authored silhouette replacement for the two north shafts and broken span, within their existing world anchors and visual bounds. That should change their broad buttress/crown/arch shapes, rather than repeat the now-accepted material treatment. Neither follow-up has been implemented in this review.
