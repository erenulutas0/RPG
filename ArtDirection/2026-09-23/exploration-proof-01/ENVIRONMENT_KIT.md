# Explorable foundry environment kit

The owner approved continuing the wide region and asked for objects and elevation that can be reused in the game. Build separate runtime pieces, not one flattened map illustration.

## First playable surface

Keep the current flat floor authority while establishing exploration. Use fixed-scale paving, a quiet central travel area and perimeter landmarks. The larger diamond remains a geometry prototype, not the intended final map outline. Character and camera scale remain unchanged.

The first art kit should contain a low stone/brass boundary module, a furnace with a clear foot pivot, and a crane/chain silhouette outside the playable edge. Reuse those pieces around the perimeter; do not scatter apparently solid objects through valid movement positions. Furnace light must stay dimmer than enemy tells and reward crystals. Keep foreground silhouettes away from the hero's route and HUD.

Each painted piece needs a retained original source, documented sampling/pivot, shared imported texture/material, predictable sorting and device inspection at native actor scale. Decorative height is safe outside travel space. Walkable ramps need an explicit height, collision, targeting and simulation contract before they can ship; a painted staircase alone is not a ramp.

## Following gameplay slice

Author physically reached encounter locations before adding navigational clutter. Define one-time activation/completion, reward ownership, reload behaviour and boss access. Preserve current save and telemetry contracts. Do not silently reinterpret linear floor progression as exploration, change stamina, or add repeatable rewards through an art view.

## Acceptance

- Paving keeps the same shape and scale across the original hall and wide region.
- Routes remain visibly open; no fake collision obstacles appear on walkable ground.
- Actor scale, reward contrast and the attack warning hierarchy survive perimeter views.
- A device build is labelled a traversal prototype until local encounters exist.
- Real elevations are a later measured slice, not implied by scenery.
