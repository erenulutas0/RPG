## Court: accept the visible direction

The medallion now visibly consists of large radial stone sectors and a fitted outer course. The former square grid no longer crosses its centre. The two full-width approaches read as broad crosswise flags instead of thin shoulders over the same old paving. There is no visible four-piece subdivision line splitting the 12 outer stones into 48 tiny blocks. The small ordinary-stone gap at the circular setting remains quiet, with no apparent hole or stacked rim.

This difference is visible at the matched whole-map scale and clearer in forecourt/court details. The hero, chest, enemies and blue attack guide remain distinct. The ground is still restrained and non-emissive. The reference has richer irregular stone and more integrated wear; this is a useful layout/material-ownership improvement, not reference parity.

## Crane: reject the material finish before final acceptance

The new grounded mass and working aperture are present, but its large hood/top carries long blue-grey streaks. The result reads as a clean corrugated sheet-metal box beside the older painted mast, iron deck and wooden crates. It is conspicuous even at full-map scale. This is a concrete material-integration problem; the geometry test passing does not make the appearance acceptable.

Required bounded correction: retain the seated body/aperture and unchanged footprint, but map the large faces to appropriately scaled interior iron grain, removing clamped/elongated striping and restraining the cold highlight family. Recheck against the existing mast and nearby boiler iron in actual captures. Do not darken the whole scene, add ornament, or enlarge the noncolliding footprint to hide the mismatch.
