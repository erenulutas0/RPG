# Next visual priorities

Planning only, based on the inspected full-map comparison with the approved reference. No new feature, navigation change or implementation is authorized by this note itself. Finish the current regression gate before beginning another package.

The next useful package should change how the whole foundry reads, rather than adding another set of small stone seams. These three related tasks have the best visual return while retaining current walkable geometry, camera, combat and progression.

1. **Give the open court a coherent large-scale material pattern.** Preserve the central fighting space, current stone ownership and brass routes. Introduce broad, low-contrast differences between the worn entry route, working apron and calmer centre, coordinated across existing stones rather than darkening individual tiles. Improve the scale of slate grain and restrained light-catching wear so the large floor reads as constructed stone at full-map scale, not a smooth flat field. Reuse the established material pipeline; avoid scattered objects, painted fake obstacles or new cracks competing with threats. Acceptance: an observable improvement in the whole court, with the hero and damage cues still clear in matched portrait views.

2. **Make existing work areas read as purposeful clusters.** Use the current crane/stock, pit machinery and gate-furnace areas as three distinct working groups. Within their existing blocked or decorative footprints, refine broad equipment shapes, linked supports, pipe routing and a few integrated stores. Connect each group through coherent local contact shadow and warm/cool material treatment. Keep the central court and walking lanes empty. Avoid isolated small clutter spread over playable floor, invisible collision or a larger collision contract. Acceptance: a full-map viewer can identify three occupied working areas and the route between them without needing a close-up.

3. **Unify the gate and furnace architecture with the painted facade.** Treat the entire gate landmark and adjacent machinery as one lighting/material family. Bring the procedural flanks' broad stone value, scale and wear closer to the central facade; give existing recesses, roof bearings, large metal shoulders and fire-facing stone a consistent depth hierarchy using the established unlit geometry/vertex-light workflow. Preserve exterior silhouettes and real openings. This is a coordinated material/lighting pass, not more ornament or another uniform exposure adjustment. Acceptance: at gate-detail and full-map scales, the door no longer looks like a more detailed insert surrounded by unrelated smooth blocks; flames remain localized highlights.

Use a single matched Before/After package with full-map, court, three work-area details and representative portrait approaches. Judge the broad visual change first, then inspect surface ownership and runtime cost. Static counts do not replace physical-device motion, occlusion and frame-time checks. The remaining reference gap includes composition and authored detail; no small package should be described as complete parity.
