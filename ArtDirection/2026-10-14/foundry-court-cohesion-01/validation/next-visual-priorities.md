# Next combined visual package

Planning only. Based on the actual After `full-map`, `yard-detail`, `casting-detail` and `crane-detail` captures, compared with `ArtDirection/2026-09-23/foundry-layout-01/foundry-v1.png`. Finish the current verification and independent acceptance before beginning another source change. These are Editor views, not phone evidence.

The current package gives the floor a broader material hierarchy and brings the bench/crane into a more consistent iron-and-timber family. The bench gain is primarily visible close up; its stored tooling does not substantially change the full-map composition. The reference still has more convincingly assembled large masonry, richer transitions between machinery and its foundations, and stronger separation of nearby architecture from the background. Repeated small seams or more loose props will not close that gap.

The next useful package is **secondary architecture and bearing surfaces**, with three bounded parts:

1. **Resolve the two native service towers behind the casting/stock pocket.** In `casting-detail` and `full-map`, their broad regular wall fields and square open crowns still contrast with the more authored painted architecture. Refine the existing large bays, shoulders and crown bearings as coherent structures, and coordinate their mineral scale/value with the backdrop. Start with `FoundryRegionDistantWorks.cs` (`DistantFurnaceShaft`, `DistantFurnaceHead`, `FinishDistantStone`); retain the established tower stations, top/foot levels, broken-span contacts, atmosphere and scenery sort ranks. Replace existing faces where needed; do not add a second wall or a field of tiny trim. Preserve the existing distant budget and test the actual visible openings, bearings and UVs with `FoundryServiceShaftTests`.

2. **Make the working shelves read as one supported architectural edge.** The stock and casting machinery is detailed, but the shelf cap/vertical masonry transition remains simpler and more regular. Refine the broad bearing course, underside haunch and material contact, preserving the rail and all scenic footprints. Scope `FoundryRegionWorkshop.cs` (`ServiceShelfFaces`, `ServiceShelfCorbel`) and `FoundryRegionCastingBench.cs` (`CastingShelfBearings`), with `FoundryLoadingSupportTests`. Use the existing stone/light pipeline; keep the baked equipment source and placement fixed. Judge the complete casting/stock pocket, not an isolated corbel enlargement.

3. **Carry that masonry family onto the near island and bridge supports.** The full-map entrance currently ends in fairly uniform large native wall planes against the much richer background. Refine existing broad bearing blocks, arch returns and contact depth using `FoundryRegionUndercroft.cs`, `FoundryRegionBridgeSupports.cs` and only the relevant surface helper in `FoundryRegionFortressSurface.cs`. Preserve real openings, deck edges and the empty playable court. Keep `FoundryUndercroftStoneTests`, `FoundryBridgeArchitectureTests` and whole-scene ownership checks authoritative. Prefer replacing coarse existing planes over adding ornament or changing the island silhouette.

Use one fresh matched Before/After set: full map, casting/stock, both service shafts, bridge/forecourt and the same representative actor approaches. Decide whether the large native structures belong to the same world as the painted reference before accepting close-up surface detail. Preserve current region/distant ceilings and resource ownership; any new texture/material needs explicit measured accounting rather than being hidden inside an art label.

No phone work is included in this proposal. Do not install, restart a run or touch saves/telemetry. Gameplay, navigation, collision, camera and regional progression remain unchanged. Reference parity remains an unfinished art goal; this package would address specific large-surface gaps, not establish that the entire reference has been reproduced.
